Sockets, gems, runes and charms

The reason to pick up a plain white item. Sockets only ever appear on basic, tierless equipment - so the cheapest thing in the game is also the raw material of the best thing in the game.

Where sockets come from

  • of qualifying drops arrive socketed.
  • Only basic quality equipment qualifies - magic, rare, set, unique and primal items never roll sockets. Every base tier does, though: a Torment base is the better host, not a worse one.
  • Jewelry is the exception. Rings and amulets have no basic versions at all, so they socket at magic, rare, unique and primal instead. Being 1x1, they get exactly one.
  • All thirteen worn slots can host them.

How many

An item's socket ceiling is the number of 28x28 inventory cells it occupies - the same footprint the backpack grid uses to place it, so the cap can never disagree with what you see.

FootprintMax socketsTypical hosts
1x11rings, amulets
1x2 / 2x12gloves, boots, belts, bracers, small weapons
2x24shields, helms, shoulders, most one-handers
2x36body armour, two-handers, staves

The count is weighted from one socket to six, then truncated at the item's own ceiling and renormalised - so a 1x2 glove rolls one against two, rather than rolling six and having every high draw clamped down onto two. A full-footprint roll on a large item stays an event.

Insertion is permanent. Drop a gem or rune onto a socketed item in your backpack and it goes in. There is no removal, so the empty socket is the decision, not the filled one.

The seven gems

Numbers are the normal quality of each type, which is the tuned row; the other four qualities are this row scaled. Where Diablo II uses a channel this engine does not have - cold, poison, damage to undead, life steal - the effect moves to the nearest channel that exists, and the substitution is named in the last column.

Five qualities

Each type comes in five grades, and a grade is a straight percentage of the row above.

QualityScaleExample - a Ruby in a weapon

Scaling never rounds a real effect away: a chipped stone with a small number still gives 1. Perfect is exactly double normal, which is where Diablo II's own perfect values land.

The thirty-three runes

All of Diablo II's runes, in D2's own order. Runes have no quality ladder - a rune is a rune - and they carry D2's own numbers wherever this engine has the channel. Seven needed no substitute at all: knockback, faster attack, faster hit recovery, fast block, thorns, life and mana steal, and triple damage to demons are real effects here. The rest name their substitution in the last column.

The ladder maps D2's own span onto this game's 1-96 area levels, so El is a first-floor find and Zod is a deep Torment one. Value climbs 13% a rung, which is what makes the ascend recipe a real ladder rather than a formality.

Two runes act on the item, not on you. Hel cuts the host's own strength, magic and dexterity requirements by 20% - they stack, capped at 60%, and what the item panel prints is what the game actually checks. Zod makes the host indestructible.

Runewords

A runeword is not a recipe you have to look up - every rune's own description lists the words it belongs to, so the recipes drop with the ingredients. Fill a socketed item of the right host with the exact runes, in the exact order, with no socket left empty, and the item takes the word's name.

WordHostRunes, in orderGrants

Runeword state is derived from what is in the sockets rather than stored, so it cannot desync and it costs the save format nothing.

Charms

Charms work from your backpack; they are never equipped. Only the first in reading order are live - main backpack first, then the extra tabs in order - which is the whole answer to Diablo II's charm-rack inventory.
