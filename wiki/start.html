Getting started

If you have played Diablo, most of this will feel familiar. These are the things that are different enough to catch you out, in the order you will meet them.

1. You start with nothing

No weapon, no armour, no potions, no gold. Both mouse buttons are on your bare fist, and your first fight is a fist fight. This is deliberate - the first weapon you pick up is an event again.

If you would rather not, set Naked Heroes=0 in diablo.ini before creating the character. It is read once, at creation, so changing it later does nothing for an existing hero.

2. Spend your first skill point

You get one skill point per level. Press D for the Abilities window: the first sheet is your spells, the other four are your class tree - three skill pages and a page of passives.

  • Left-click a skill to spend a point in it. Right-click takes one back, free, one rank at a time. There are no plus and minus buttons - the cell itself is the control.
  • A grey plate means the skill is yours but empty - spend a point and it lights gold. A red plate means you have not earned it yet, and a green one is a passive you have assigned to a slot. A red X means the skill is listed but not built yet - it takes nothing and does nothing.
  • Tier-1 skills are available at character level 1. See Class trees.

3. Put something on each mouse button

Left-click a skill to put it on the left button; right-click to put it on the right. Auras go to the right button whichever button you click them with.

The left button always interacts first. Clicking a door, an item or a corpse does that, not your skill - so a skill on the left button is safe to leave there.

To get the plain attack back on a button, click that button's well on the HUD and pick it from the strip that appears.

5. Depth is the whole progression

Each difficulty is 16 rungs of one continuous 64-rung ladder, walked over 24 floors - the Nest and the Crypt are side-steps that share the Caves' and Hell's rungs rather than going deeper. Almost everything keys off that rung: which bases can drop, which tier they are forged at, how good the rolls are, which spell books exist. By Hell/Hell every base item in the game is obtainable; past that you are farming for better rolls rather than new things.

6. Spells have a level band

Every spell demands a character level before it can be learned: . A book you cannot read is a book that will not drop, so you will not find Apocalypse in the Cathedral.

Deepening a spell follows the Rule of Rangs: each rank costs one more character level than the last, up to a ceiling of 30 ranks - the same cap every skill and aura has. Depth is paid for in character levels, and no one skill can swallow a whole career's points.

8. Things that will surprise a Diablo veteran

  • No Item Repair, Trap Disarm, Search, Identify, Staff Recharge or the old Rage skill. The vanilla class skills are retired; the trees replaced them. Griswold repairs, Adria recharges, Cain identifies.
  • The Barbarian has no mana. He spends Rage, which his blows build and which drains once he stops fighting - his orb and every skill description say Rage where the others say mana.
  • Summons are Companions. The Valkyrie, the three Ancients, the Spirit Guardian and the Decoy stand beside you for a time, keep formation, fight for a share of your own damage, and come back with you on the next level. J sets what they do.
  • An aura and a right-button skill are one slot. Lighting an aura clears the skill; readying a skill puts the aura out.
  • Torment is a fourth difficulty above Hell, single-player only, with its own multiplier.
  • The belt has four potion slots, with a Menu button at one end and a permanent town-portal button at the other.
  • The inventory has ten tabs and thirteen equipment slots, including shoulders, bracers, gloves, belt, legs and boots.
  • Plain white items are the endgame material. A quarter of basic-quality equipment drops socketed, at any base tier, and gems, runes and runewords all live in those sockets. Rings and amulets are the exception - having no white versions, they socket at magic and better. See Sockets and gems.
  • There is no Continue and no save button. Picking a character always starts a fresh dungeon; the character is what persists. The game autosaves on its own - see Saving and progress.
  • Press R to run, anywhere, not just in town. No stamina.
  • Skill points come back. One rank at a time for free, or the whole pool at Adria for gold.
  • Gold stacks far higher than vanilla's cap.
