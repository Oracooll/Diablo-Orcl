## The measurement first

The user then asked whether the composite and the asset were the same size. They are not, and the
screenshot answered it exactly:

**The screenshot is 1:1 with the game.** The gilded sarcophagus profiles at 91 px across in it
against a known `OracoolStashChestAnimWidth` of 90, so on-screen pixels are game pixels.

| | width |
|---|---|
| The cart in the composite | **157 px** (columns 265..421) |
| The CEL built here | **160 px** |
| The raw asset | 209x209, ink 195x195 |

So the asset is much bigger than what was pasted, and the CEL - which is the asset cropped to its
content box and resampled - lands three pixels off the composite, inside the error of measuring a
soft alpha edge over a dark scene.

Height could not be separated: Wirt stands directly under the cart in that column strip and his
skin and hair pass the same colour test.

## How it is built

`Resources\Wirt Cart.png` -> `tools\ScalePainting.ps1 -Width 160` -> `tools\FramesCel.cs`
quantised onto `tools\town.pal` -> `objects\orclcart.cel`, one frame, 160x157. The same pipeline
the Rift Monument uses, with `tools\build_wirt_cart_cel.cmd` recording the recipe and the
width-must-match warning that CEL's format makes necessary.

NO toning, unlike the monument: this is a painted wooden cart meant to read as a merchant's stall,
not as Tristram's blue rock.

## What it is in the engine

An `OBJ_STAND` wearing its own art - how the Roar, the Stonegate and the Stonegate's portal arch
are all built - and **pure scenery**: `_oSelFlag = 0`, not solid, missiles pass, no name, no click.
`_oPreFlag` puts it in the before-characters pass so Wirt and the hero walk in front of it rather
than being swallowed by a cart twice their height.

It is an object at all only because objects are what the town draws in TILE order. A cart blitted
at a screen position would sit in front of the hero on one step and behind him on the next.

## The trap it walked into

`IsLevskiRoarObject` asks *"a town OBJ_STAND that is not the gate"* to mean *"is this Levski's
Cube"*. A fourth stand in town makes a cart **clickable as the Cube**, and `SyncObjectAnim`'s
matching branch - which has never named the gate either - would repaint it with the monument's
sprite on the first town reload.

The cart has its own identity (`WirtCartObjectId` / `IsWirtCartObject`, re-established on every
town entry exactly as `GateObjectId` is) and is named in both places.

That is now the **third exception** on a test that should say what it IS looking for. It is not
inverted here because the Cube's identity would have to survive a town reload, and that is a change
to the Cube rather than to a cart. Both sites carry the warning.

## The tile

**(52, 71)**, two steps up-left and one up-right of Wirt at (54, 72) - about 32 px left and 96 px
up, which is where the painting sits in the composite. Neighbouring tiles as fallbacks, in the
order that keeps it near him, and a logged event if none is free.

Eyeballed from a composite rather than from the game, so this is the number here most likely to
want moving - as the Rift Monument's did ("Move the Rift Monument to 31:56 tile").

## Open: the roof quantised green

Decoding the built CEL back through the town palette shows the thatched roof reading **green**
where the source is warm straw. Everything else - the wood, the wares, the lanterns, the barrel -
survives closely.

That is the palette, not the cut: the roof's olive-tan lands on the town palette's mossy entries.
The fix is a warm tint before quantising, which `ScalePainting.ps1` already supports (`-TintRgb`
/ `-TintStrength`, as the Rift Monument's blue-slate cast uses). Left for the user to judge against
the town rather than guessed at.
