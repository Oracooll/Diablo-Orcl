# 1.8.26 - A cube that knows how big things are

> "why are boxes in Levski so big? Why do they fit a whole armor in one? All this makes 0 sense."

Correct on both counts, and the second is the real one.

## What was wrong

The grid was twelve **56x56** boxes holding one item each, footprint ignored. That is wrong twice:

1. **The box never fitted what it claimed to.** 56px is two inventory cells square. A 2x3 armour is
   56x84. The sprite was being centred into a box it overflowed, then quietly drawn anyway.
2. **Capacity meant nothing.** A rune and a breastplate both counted as "one box", so a 3x4 cube
   held twelve breastplates. Nothing about the container pushed back on what went in it.

The comment justifying 56px said *"two inventory cells, so a 2x3 two-hander still reads in a slot"* -
which is the arithmetic failing out loud, in writing, since 2x3 is not 2x2. Written once, never
re-read.

I also reached for the wrong references when describing the rebuild, and the user was right to
reject them: **Kanai's Cube has no grid at all** (it is a recipe list with named slots), and the
Horadric Cube is a plain 3x4 of *ordinary inventory cells*. The chosen model is the latter.

## What it is now

A real item grid: **3 columns x 4 rows of 28x28 inventory cells**, items occupying their true
`GetInventorySize` footprint. One armour fills half the cube. Twelve runes fill it exactly.

Two arrays, shaped like `Player::InvGrid` on purpose so the rules are the ones the player already
knows from the backpack and the stash:

- `GridItems[12]` - indexed by the item's **top-left** cell.
- `GridCells[12]` - the anchor's index + 1 for every cell an item covers, 0 for free.

Behaviour that follows from it:

- The clicked cell is the item's top-left. If the footprint runs off the grid or over something
  else, placement falls back to the first cell where it does fit, rather than refusing.
- **Any covered cell lifts the item**, not just its anchor - otherwise the bottom half of every
  breastplate is dead surface.
- The cell size is the inventory's cell, exactly. Item sprites are cut to a whole number of 28px
  cells, so any other size crops them or leaves them swimming.

## The transmute problem this created

The recipes rewrite `GridItems` in place - three gems become one, a socketed item becomes an item
plus its stones - with no idea of footprints, and the outputs are not the size of the inputs. So
after every transmute the occupancy map is **re-derived, not patched**: collect what is there, clear,
re-place largest-first (a 2x3 placed after four runes may find no run of free cells left, while the
runes always fit around it). Anchors may move, which is correct. The same rebuild runs after a
partial return-to-backpack, where some items stay behind.

## Presentation

The empty grid is drawn as one recessed well with cell lines on it, rather than twelve individually
bordered 28px boxes - at this size, twelve borders read as noise instead of as a container. The
window is now sized to the wider of the grid and its own buttons, with the grid centred, since three
28px cells is 84px and narrower than the word "Transmute".

## Verification

Debug build clean. **454 tests, the usual two** (`Drlg_l1.CreateL5Dungeon_diablo_3_844660068`,
`Timedemo.WarriorLevel1to2`).

Still unfixed and still open from 1.8.25: Ctrl+Click gold to stash, awaiting one datapoint - whether
Ctrl+Click moves a *non-gold* item to the stash.

## To look at in game

1. Drop an armour in: it should occupy 2x3 cells, and a second one should not fit.
2. Drop runes in: twelve should fit, one per cell.
3. Click the bottom half of a large item - it should lift.
4. Transmute with a mix of sizes in the grid and check nothing overlaps afterwards.
